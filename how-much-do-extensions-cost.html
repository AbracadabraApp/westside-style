HOW MUCH DO EXTENSIONS COST?

It depends on the method, hair amount, and length you want.

WHAT AFFECTS THE COST

Extension type. Keratin bonds require more labor and time than tape-in or hand-tied, which affects installation cost.

Amount of hair needed. Adding fullness to thin hair requires less hair than a full head of long extensions. More hair = higher cost.

Hair quality. Premium human hair lasts longer and looks more natural, but costs more upfront. Lower-grade hair is cheaper but doesn't last as long.

Length. Longer hair (18"+ inches) costs more than shorter lengths because it's more difficult to source and process.

INITIAL INSTALLATION

Installation includes the cost of the hair itself plus the service fee for application and styling.

Keratin bonds typically have the highest initial cost due to the time-intensive application process — each strand is applied individually.

Hand-tied and tape-in installations are faster, which generally means lower initial service costs, though the hair quality still affects overall price.

MAINTENANCE COSTS

Move-up appointments are required every 6-8 weeks for hand-tied and tape-in extensions. These cost significantly less than the initial install since you're reusing the same hair.

Keratin bonds last 4-6 months before removal, so you have fewer appointments. When it's time to replace them, you can often reuse the same hair if it's still in good condition.

Budget for sulfate-free shampoo and conditioner, a quality brush for extensions, and occasional deep conditioning treatments.

LONG-TERM VALUE

Quality extensions are an investment. Lower-cost options may seem appealing, but they tangle, shed, and need replacement sooner — costing more in the long run.

Premium human hair can be reused multiple times. Hand-tied and tape-in hair lasts a year or longer with proper care. Keratin bonds can be reused 2-3 times.

When you consider how long quality extensions last and how much confidence they provide, the cost per day is often less than you'd expect.
